Romania

Romania is an Eastern European country (EU member since 2007) with its capital at Bucharest. It has about 19 million inhabitants and covers 238,398 km².

Its official language is Romanian; the currency is the Romanian leu (RON). Major industries include manufacturing (auto, machinery), IT services, construction, agriculture, and tourism.

Visa Categories & Application Process

Nepali citizens need a national long‑stay (Type D) visa for employment, often labeled D/Am (work).

  • Permanent/Regular worker Employed under an indefinite or fixed-term contract.
  • Highly qualified (EU Blue Card) For workers with a university degree hired for highly skilled jobs. This also requires a work permit but offers EU-Blue-Card–like status.
  • Seasonal worker For work tied to seasonal industries (agriculture, tourism). This visa is tied to a fixed-term contract.
  • Intra-company posted worker For qualified employees temporarily sent by a foreign-based employer (deployment visa).

Nepalese applicants typically pursue the standard work visa (with permit); Blue Card is an option if highly qualified.

Eligibility & Legal Basis

All non-EU citizens need a Romanian work permit before obtaining a work visa. The employer applies for a work permit at Romania’s General Inspectorate for Immigration (IGI). Requirements include: having a valid job offer and employment contract, meeting training/experience conditions, the position not being fillable by EU/EEA locals, and falling under the annual quota. The work permit rules are set by Law 202/2002 and subsequent regulations. Once approved, Nepali workers apply for the national work visa at the Romanian embassy/consulate.

Fees & Government Charges

The employer pays a work-permit fee of €100 (in RON) for a permanent/high-skilled permit (seasonal: €25). The visa application fee is €120. Other costs include medical exam fees, document translations, and travel. (Approximate total cost: visa €120 + medical ~€20 + translations/apostille ~€50–100, plus airfare.)

Application Steps

  • 1. Job Search & Offer Nepali applicant secures a job offer from a Romanian employer.
  • 2. Work Permit Employer submits the permit application (with contract, vacancy notices, etc.) to IGI; processed in ~30 days (plus 15-day extension if needed).
  • 3. Visa Appointment Once permit is issued, applicant applies for the D/Am visa. In Nepal this may involve the Romanian embassy in New Delhi or an honorary consulate. Required documents are submitted.
  • 4. Visa Decision The embassy/consulate issues the visa (valid multi-entry for 90 days to pick up the residence permit). Processing often takes 30–60 days.
  • 5. Travel & Registration Worker travels to Romania and applies for a temporary residence/work permit (“card”) within 90 days of arrival.

Documents Checklist

To apply, Nepali citizens must prepare (with Romanian translations and legalization):

  • Passport: Valid ≥ 3 months beyond stay, plus copies.
  • Photos: Two passport-size.
  • Work Permit: Copy of IGI-issued work permit or permit approval letter.
  • Employment Contract/Offer: Signed job contract.
  • Criminal Record: Police clearance certificate from Nepal (apostilled by Nepali authorities, translated).
  • Medical Certificate: A health certificate showing fitness/no contagious diseases (must be legalized/translated).
  • Medical Insurance: Proof of travel/health insurance valid in Romania for visa duration.
  • Proof of Funds: In practice, employers often provide proof of sufficient salary (≥ Romania’s minimum wage).
  • Accommodation: Address in Romania (hotel booking or company-provided lodging).
  • Qualifications: Degrees or certificates (apostilled and translated) if job requires (especially for professionals).
  • Filled Visa Form: National visa application form (embassy website).
Note: All foreign documents must typically be legalized with an Apostille (Nepal is party to the Hague Convention) and translated into Romanian (or English) by an authorized translator.

Romanian Work Culture

Romanian work culture combines professionalism with a friendly and approachable environment. The official language is Romanian, but English is widely used in sectors like IT, finance, and multinational companies, while blue-collar jobs mostly require Romanian.

The standard work schedule is 8 hours per day and 5 days a week, totaling 40 hours, with overtime allowed under legal limits. Employees are entitled to paid leave and public holidays, and working on holidays requires extra pay or compensatory leave.

Teamwork and flexibility are highly valued, especially in modern workplaces. Although companies may follow a hierarchical structure, many encourage ideas from junior employees. Communication in Romania is often direct, so clarity is important.

Nepali workers should be respectful, punctual, and reliable, as these qualities are highly appreciated. Learning basic Romanian phrases can help in daily interactions. It is also important to understand labor laws, as employees usually get at least 20 days of annual leave and standard worker rights are protected.
